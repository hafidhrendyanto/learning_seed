# Nondepository Institutions

Nondepository institutions facilitate financial intermediation but **do not accept traditional deposits**. They raise funds through other means such as selling shares, collecting premiums, or issuing securities.

## Types

### Finance Companies
- Raise funds by issuing securities
- Lend to individuals and small businesses
- Focus on specific market segments

### Mutual Funds
- Sell shares to surplus units
- Invest in diversified portfolios of securities
- Allow small savers to access diversified investments
- Package hundreds of securities into a single affordable investment (e.g., a small investor can own a slice of 100+ companies for as little as $10)
- Types: stock funds, bond funds, money market funds

### Securities Firms
- **Brokerage Services** — Execute transactions for commission
- **Dealer Services** — Make markets by maintaining inventories
- **Investment Banking** — Underwrite new securities, advise on mergers

### Insurance Companies
- Collect premiums for risk coverage
- Invest premiums in stocks and bonds
- Channel funds to deficit units through investments

### Pension Funds
- Manage retirement contributions from employees and employers
- Invest in stocks, corporate bonds, and government bonds
- Channel retirement savings to finance deficit units
- Long-term focus until individuals withdraw for retirement

## Key Distinction from Depository Institutions

While nondepository institutions collect funds (premiums, contributions, share purchases), these are **not deposits** in the legal sense:
- No deposit insurance
- Different regulatory frameworks
- Different liquidity and access terms

The fundamental difference is **liquidity and risk-bearing**:
- **Bank deposits** are demandable debt claims — you can withdraw cash whenever you want, and the bank bears the investment risk.
- **Mutual fund shares** must be sold in the market — you receive the current market value, which fluctuates, and you bear the investment risk directly.
