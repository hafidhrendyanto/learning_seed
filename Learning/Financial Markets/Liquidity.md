# Liquidity

Liquidity refers to the degree to which securities can be easily sold (liquidated) without loss of value.

## Why Liquidity Matters

- Highly liquid securities are preferred because they offer **flexibility**
- Investors can quickly convert assets to cash when needed
- Especially important for **long-term securities** like bonds

## Factors Affecting Liquidity

- **Trading volume**: More buyers and sellers = more liquid
- **Market depth**: Availability of orders at various prices
- **Information availability**: More transparency = more participation

## Examples of Liquid Assets

- **Treasury securities**: Among the most liquid due to frequent issuance and strong demand
- **Large-cap stocks**: Companies like Apple, Microsoft trade millions of shares daily
- **Money market securities**: Very short-term, highly liquid

## Liquidity and Secondary Markets

The [[Secondary Market]] is what creates liquidity. Without it, investors would be stuck holding securities until maturity.
