# Role of Financial Markets

Financial markets are platforms where financial assets (such as stocks and bonds) are bought and sold. Their core purpose is [[Financial Intermediation|financial intermediation]] — connecting those with excess funds to those who need funds.

## Key Functions

- **Fund Flow**: Move money from [[Surplus and Deficit Units|surplus units]] to [[Surplus and Deficit Units|deficit units]]
- **Payment Systems**: Enable smooth transactions throughout the economy
- **Risk Management**: Provide tools and instruments to manage financial risk

## Types of Securities Traded

Markets facilitate trading of:
- [[Debt Securities]]
- [[Equity Securities]]

## Market Layers

Markets operate in two main layers:
- [[Primary Market]] — where new securities are issued
- [[Secondary Market]] — where existing securities are traded

## Why Surplus and Deficit Units Don't Connect Directly

Direct lending is hindered by several market frictions that financial markets help resolve:
- **Search Costs** — Difficulty finding suitable counterparts
- **Information Asymmetry** — Hard to assess creditworthiness
- **Liquidity Mismatch** — Savers may need money back before borrowers can repay
- **Lack of Diversification** — Concentrated risk in a single loan

This is why [[Financial Intermediation|financial intermediaries]] are essential.
