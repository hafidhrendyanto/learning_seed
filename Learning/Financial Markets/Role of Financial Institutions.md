# Role of Financial Institutions

Financial institutions act as **intermediaries** that channel funds from [[Surplus and Deficit Units|surplus units]] to [[Surplus and Deficit Units|deficit units]], reducing transaction and information costs in [[Financial Markets|financial markets]].

## Core Functions

- **Intermediation** — Connect savers and borrowers
- **Risk Transformation** — Bear and diversify default risk
- **Maturity Transformation** — Convert short-term deposits into long-term loans
- **Liquidity Provision** — Offer flexible access to funds
- **Information Processing** — Specialize in evaluating creditworthiness

## Two Main Categories

### 1. Depository Institutions
Accept deposits from the public and use them to make loans and purchase securities.
- Commercial Banks
- Savings Institutions (S&Ls, Savings Banks)
- Credit Unions

### 2. Nondepository Institutions
Generate funds from sources other than deposits but also facilitate financial intermediation.
- Mutual Funds
- Insurance Companies
- Pension Funds
- Finance Companies
- Securities Firms

## Flow of Funds

```
Surplus Units ──► Financial Institutions ──► Deficit Units
(Households,       (Banks, Mutual Funds,   (Businesses,
 Pension Funds)     Insurance, Pension)      Governments)
        │                  │                      │
        └─ Deposits ───────┘                      │
        └─ Security Purchases ────────────────────┘
        └─ Premiums/Contributions ────────────────┘
```

## Why Classification Matters

While some nondepository institutions (like pension funds) collect regular contributions that *resemble* deposits, the legal structure, regulatory framework, and access rules differ fundamentally. Deposits are insured, liquid, and represent a debt claim; pension contributions are locked, uninsured, and represent a claim on future benefits.

> **Boundary case explored in session:** A pension fund collecting retirement contributions from millions of employees is still a **nondepository institution** because contributions are legally distinct from deposits — they are locked until retirement, lack deposit insurance, and are regulated under pension laws rather than banking laws.
