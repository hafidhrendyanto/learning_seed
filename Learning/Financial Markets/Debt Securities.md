# Debt Securities

Debt securities represent borrowed funds that must be repaid.

## Key Characteristics

- Provide **periodic interest payments**
- Repay **principal at maturity**
- Do not confer ownership in the issuing entity
- Legally binding obligation to pay

## Types of Debt Securities

### Money Market Securities (Short-term, ≤ 1 year)
- **Treasury Bills** — Issued by U.S. Treasury, highly liquid, low credit risk
- **Commercial Paper** — Issued by corporations to meet short-term obligations
- **Negotiable Certificates of Deposit** — Issued by depository institutions

### Capital Market Securities (Long-term)
- **Treasury Bonds** — No default risk (perceived), typically 10–20 years
- **Corporate Bonds** — Higher return but higher risk
- **Mortgages** — Loans to finance home or commercial property purchases
- **Mortgage-Backed Securities** — Claims on a package of bundled mortgages

## Why Issue Debt?

**Advantages for issuer:**
- Does not dilute ownership or control
- Interest payments are often tax-deductible

**Disadvantages for issuer:**
- Requires regular interest payments regardless of profitability
- Creates legal obligation to repay principal
- Can lead to default if cash flow is insufficient

## Comparison with Equity

| Feature | Debt Securities | [[Equity Securities]] |
|---------|----------------|----------------------|
| Ownership | No | Yes |
| Periodic Payments | Required (interest) | Optional (dividends) |
| Maturity | Fixed | No maturity |
| Risk to Investor | Lower (fixed cash flows) | Higher (uncertain returns) |
| Risk to Issuer | Default risk | Dilution risk |
