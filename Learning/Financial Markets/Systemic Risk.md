# Systemic Risk

Systemic risk refers to the potential for financial problems to spread among institutions and across markets, potentially leading to a collapse of the financial system.

## Why It Spreads

### Similar Investments
- Many institutions invest in the same types of securities
- When those assets lose value, EVERYONE is hurt simultaneously
- Example: Mortgage-backed securities in 2008

### Interconnected Obligations
- Banks and institutions lend to each other constantly
- If one fails, others who lent to it suffer losses
- Creates a network of vulnerability

### Domino Effect (Contagion)
- Panic spreads from one institution to others
- Even healthy institutions face runs if confidence evaporates
- "Fear becomes self-fulfilling"

## Real Example: Credit Crisis of 2008–2009

1. Banks made risky mortgage loans
2. Mortgages were bundled into securities sold worldwide
3. Housing prices fell, borrowers defaulted
4. Institutions holding mortgage-backed securities suffered massive losses
5. Lehman Brothers collapsed; AIG nearly failed
6. Credit froze globally — even healthy businesses couldn't borrow
7. Crisis spread across countries and asset classes

## Regulatory Responses

### Higher Capital Requirements
- Banks must hold more of their **own capital** (shareholders' equity) as a buffer
- Capital absorbs losses FIRST, before depositors are affected
- Higher capital = more "skin in the game" = more careful lending

### How Capital Absorption Works

```
Loss hits bank capital (shareholders) FIRST
Only if capital is exhausted does it hit depositors
```

| Scenario | Low Capital (3%) | High Capital (15%) |
|---|---|---|
| Cushion | Tiny | Substantial |
| Risk to depositors | High | Low |
| Incentive for careful lending | Weak | Strong |

This creates a powerful incentive effect: when banks must use more of their **own money** (shareholders' equity), they are naturally disincentivized from making poor investments — a direct response to the moral hazard created by deposit insurance.

### Other Post-2008 Measures
- **Stress Testing** — Regular assessments of banks' ability to withstand shocks
- **Enhanced Oversight** — Stricter supervision of systemically important institutions
- **Resolution Planning** — "Living wills" for orderly failure management
