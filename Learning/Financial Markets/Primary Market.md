# Primary Market

The primary market is where new securities are issued and sold for the first time.

## Key Characteristics

- Corporations and governments obtain **new funds**
- Investors make **initial investments**
- The issuer receives the money from the sale

## Examples

- **IPO (Initial Public Offering)**: A private company sells new shares to the public for the first time
- **Secondary Offering**: An already-public company issues **new** shares to raise additional capital
- **New government bonds**: Treasury issues new bonds to fund infrastructure
- **Corporate bond issuance**: A company sells new bonds to raise capital

## Retail Access

For most retail investors, primary market access is limited:
- US Treasuries: Available via TreasuryDirect.gov
- Some IPOs: Occasionally available through brokerages (Fidelity, Schwab, Robinhood IPO Access)
- Most primary issuance is restricted to institutional investors

## Relationship with Secondary Market

The [[Secondary Market]] is essential for the primary market to function. Without the ability to resell securities later, investors would be much less willing to buy new issues.

A company's **secondary offering** (new shares issued after the IPO) is priced relative to the current **secondary market price**. The higher the secondary market price, the more capital the company can raise per share.
