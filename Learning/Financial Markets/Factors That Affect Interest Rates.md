# Factors That Affect Interest Rates

Beyond the basic supply and demand for loanable funds, two major forces systematically push interest rates up or down: **monetary policy** and **government budget deficits**.

## Impact of Monetary Policy

The **Federal Reserve** (the U.S. central bank) influences interest rates by adjusting the **money supply**.

### Expansionary Policy (Lower Rates)
- The Fed **increases** the money supply
- More money available for lending → supply of loanable funds shifts right
- Result: **lower interest rates**
- Goal: stimulate borrowing, spending, and economic growth during weak conditions

### Contractionary Policy (Higher Rates)
- The Fed **reduces** the money supply
- Less money available → supply shifts left
- Result: **higher interest rates**
- Goal: combat inflation by cooling down the economy

This connects directly to the [[Loanable Funds Theory]] — the Fed shifts the **supply curve** of loanable funds.

## Impact of Budget Deficits

When the federal government spends more than it collects in taxes, it runs a **budget deficit** and must borrow to cover the gap.

### Crowding-Out Effect
- Government borrowing increases **demand** for loanable funds
- Demand curve shifts **outward**
- Result: **higher interest rates**
- This makes borrowing more expensive for **private sector** (households and businesses)
- Private investment is "crowded out" by government borrowing

### Why It Matters
Even small rate increases can be costly:
- A 1% rise on $30 million in bonds = $300,000 more in annual interest
- Excessive government demand can significantly raise rates economy-wide
