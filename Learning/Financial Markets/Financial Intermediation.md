# Financial Intermediation

Financial intermediation is the process by which funds are channeled from [[Surplus and Deficit Units|surplus units]] (savers) to [[Surplus and Deficit Units|deficit units]] (borrowers) through [[Role of Financial Markets|financial markets]] and [[Financial Institutions|financial institutions]].

## Why Intermediaries Are Needed

Direct lending is impractical for most savers due to several market frictions. A student independently identified these four core problems when asked about lending to a friend:

1. **Information Asymmetry / Credit Risk** — Lenders cannot easily verify borrower's honesty or ability to repay ("takut nggak diganti atau ditipu")
2. **Liquidity Mismatch** — Savers may need funds back before borrowers can repay ("takut ada keperluan mendesak dan aku butuh uangnya")
3. **Time Value of Money / Inflation Risk** — Money returned later has less purchasing power ("takut gantiinya lama sedangkan nilai uangnya menurun")
4. **Transaction Costs & Scale Problems** — Individual savers lack sufficient capital and expertise to diversify across many borrowers ("ribet, ga ada duit buat minjemin ke 100 pengusaha")

Formal terms:
- **Search Costs** — Difficulty finding suitable borrowers or lenders
- **Credit Evaluation** — Requires expertise to assess default risk
- **Liquidity Transformation** — Savers want flexible access; borrowers need long-term capital
- **Diversification** — Pooling enables spreading risk across many loans
- **Transaction Costs** — Managing contracts, payments, and enforcement is costly at small scale

## What Intermediaries Do

- Accept deposits or sell shares to surplus units
- Evaluate and monitor borrowers
- Transform short-term, liquid deposits into long-term, illiquid loans
- Bear default risk on behalf of savers
- Provide payment and settlement services

## Why It Matters

Without intermediation:
- Savers would struggle to find suitable investments
- Borrowers would struggle to find funding
- Economic growth would slow down
