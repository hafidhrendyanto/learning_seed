# Surplus and Deficit Units

These are the two key types of participants that drive the flow of funds in [[Role of Financial Markets|financial markets]].

## Surplus Units

- **Definition**: Participants who receive more money than they spend
- **Role**: Provide net savings to [[Role of Financial Markets|financial markets]]
- **Goal**: Earn a return on their investment
- **Examples**: 
  - Households with income exceeding expenses
  - Pension funds collecting retirement contributions
  - Some corporations with excess cash
  - **Commercial banks** (primarily intermediaries, but can act as surplus units in markets like the federal funds market)

## Deficit Units

- **Definition**: Participants who spend more money than they receive
- **Role**: Access funds from [[Role of Financial Markets|financial markets]] to cover their deficit
- **Goal**: Fund investments or large purchases that generate future value
- **Examples**: 
  - Businesses needing capital for expansion
  - Entrepreneurs starting new ventures
  - Households borrowing for homes or cars
  - Governments running budget deficits

## Key Insight: Stock vs. Flow

Surplus/deficit status is about **flow** (money received vs. spent over time), **not stock** (total wealth).

- A **wealthy person** can be a **deficit unit** when undertaking a large investment (e.g., a property developer with Rp 100 billion in assets building a Rp 150 billion project)
- A **modest earner** can be a **surplus unit** by consistently spending less than their income

> **Student insight**: Someone who inherits Rp 10 billion becomes a deficit unit when building a Rp 50 billion factory, even though they are wealthy.

Being a deficit unit is not necessarily bad — it depends on whether the borrowed funds are used productively (e.g., investing in education, building a factory) versus unproductive consumption.

## Why They Don't Connect Directly

Direct lending between surplus and deficit units is hindered by:
- **Search Costs** — Difficulty finding suitable counterparts
- **Information Asymmetry** — Hard to assess creditworthiness
- **Liquidity Mismatch** — Savers may need money back before borrowers can repay
- **Lack of Diversification** — Concentrated risk in a single loan

This is why [[Role of Financial Markets|financial markets]] and [[Financial Institutions|financial institutions]] are essential intermediaries.
