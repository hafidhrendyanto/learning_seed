## Overview of Financial Markets

- Financial markets provide for financial intermediation:
  - Financial savings (Surplus Units) → Investment (Deficit Units)
  - Provide payment systems
  - Provide means to manage risk

**Definition:**  
Financial Market is a market in which financial assets (securities) such as stocks and bonds can be purchased or sold.

---

# 01 — Role of Financial Markets

## Financial Markets: Facilitating Fund Flows

### Transfer of Funds
Financial markets enable the transfer of funds from parties with excess funds to those who need funds.

### Financing and Investing
They allow for financing and investing by households, firms, and government agencies.

### Return on Investment
Surplus units supply funds to financial markets to earn a return on their investment.

---

## Surplus and Deficit Units

### Surplus Units
Participants who receive more money than they spend, providing net savings to financial markets.

### Deficit Units
Participants who spend more money than they receive, accessing funds from financial markets to cover their deficit.

---

## Securities in Financial Markets

### Debt Securities
Represent borrowed funds, providing periodic interest payments and principal repayment at maturity.

### Equity Securities
Represent ownership in a firm, often preferred by businesses unable to make periodic interest payments.

---

## Corporate Finance and Investment Management

### Corporate Finance
Involves decisions on how much funding to obtain and which types of securities to issue.

### Financial Markets
Serve as the mechanism for corporations to obtain funds from investors.

### Investment Management
Involves decisions by investors on how to invest their funds in various securities.

---

## Accommodating Corporate Finance Needs

The financial markets serve as the mechanism whereby corporations (deficit units) obtain funds from investors (surplus units).

---

## Accommodating Investment Needs

Financial institutions serve as intermediaries to connect investment management activity with corporate finance activity.

---

## Primary vs Secondary Markets

### Primary Markets
Facilitate the issuance of new securities:
- Corporations obtain new funds
- Investors make initial investments

### Secondary Markets
Facilitate trading of existing securities:
- Investors buy/sell securities
- Allows portfolio adjustment

---

## Liquidity in Financial Markets

**Definition:**  
Liquidity refers to the degree to which securities can be easily liquidated without loss of value.

### Importance
Highly liquid securities are preferred for flexibility.

### Long-term Securities
Liquidity is especially important for long-term debt securities.

### Treasury Securities
Known for high liquidity due to frequent issuance and strong demand.

---

# 02 — Securities Traded in Financial Markets

## Types of Securities

### 1. Money Market Securities
Short-term debt securities with maturity ≤ 1 year.

### 2. Capital Market Securities
Long-term securities:
- Bonds
- Mortgages
- Stocks

### 3. Derivative Securities
Financial contracts whose value is derived from underlying assets.

---

## Money Market Securities

Short-term debt instruments with:
- High liquidity
- Low credit risk

### Types:
- Treasury Bills → Issued by U.S. Treasury  
- Commercial Paper → Issued by corporations  
- Negotiable Certificates of Deposit → Issued by depository institutions  

---

## Capital Market Securities: Bonds

Bonds are long-term debt securities that:
- Provide periodic interest payments
- Repay principal at maturity

### Types:
- Treasury Bonds → No default risk (perceived)
- Corporate Bonds → Higher return, higher risk

### Maturity
Typically 10–20 years.

---

## Capital Market Securities: Mortgages

### Residential Mortgages
- Finance home purchases
- Require creditworthiness assessment

### Commercial Mortgages
- Finance commercial properties
- Used by developers (e.g., offices, malls)

---

## Mortgage-Backed Securities

Debt obligations representing claims on a package of mortgages.

### Process:
1. Mortgage Origination → Banks issue mortgages  
2. Securitization → Mortgages bundled  
3. Investment → Investors buy securities  
4. Payment Flow → Payments distributed to investors  

---

## Capital Market Securities: Stocks

Stocks represent partial ownership in corporations.

### Key Aspects:
- Ownership → Equity stake  
- Returns → Dividends + capital gains  
- Liquidity → Depends on firm size & trading volume  

---

## Derivative Securities

Derivative securities are financial contracts whose values are derived from underlying assets.

### Roles:
- **Speculation**
  - Investors can speculate on asset price movements without owning the asset.
- **Risk Management**
  - Used to hedge against potential losses in asset values.

---

## Valuation of Securities

The value of a security is the **present value of expected future cash flows**, discounted based on risk.

### Debt Securities
- Easier to value
- Have fixed and known cash flows

### Stocks
- Harder to value
- Future cash flows are uncertain

### Financial Statements
- Investors use financial reports to estimate future cash flows

---

## Impact of Information on Valuation

1. **New Information**
   - Investors receive new information about a security

2. **Revised Valuation**
   - Investors update their valuation

3. **Demand Change**
   - Demand shifts based on updated valuation

4. **Price Adjustment**
   - Market price moves to new equilibrium

---

## Efficient Markets

In an efficient market:
- Prices reflect all available public information

### Key Characteristics:
- **Information Accessibility**
  - Easy access to price and transaction data

- **Online Trading**
  - Speeds up reaction to new information

- **Price Correction**
  - Investor actions correct mispricing

---

## Behavioral Finance

Applies psychology to financial decision-making.

### Key Concepts:
- **Investor Psychology**
  - Explains irrational decisions

- **Market Inefficiencies**
  - Markets are not always perfectly efficient

- **Price Bubbles**
  - Explains formation and bursting of bubbles

---

## Uncertainty in Security Valuation

Valuation is uncertain due to limited and asymmetric information. The valuation of securities is subject to uncertainty due to limited information and 
different interpretations by investors. This uncertainty contributes to the risk 
associated with investing in securities.

### Factors:
- **Asymmetric Information**
  - Managers know more than investors

- **Interpretation Differences**
  - Investors interpret the same info differently

- **Risk**
  - More uncertainty → higher risk

---

## Securities Regulations on Financial Disclosure

### 1. Securities Act of 1933
- Requires full disclosure for new securities

### 2. Securities Exchange Act of 1934
- Extends disclosure to secondary markets
- Established the SEC

### 3. Sarbanes-Oxley Act of 2002
- Strengthens auditing and financial reporting oversight

---

## International Financial Markets

Financial markets are continuously developing worldwide, facilitating the transfer 
of securities between surplus and deficit units. The level of development and 
liquidity varies across countries.

### Influencing Factors:
- **Financial Disclosure**
  - Countries with more disclosure tend to have more liquid markets
  - More transparency → more liquidity

- **Shareholder Rights**
  - Vary by country, influencing investor participation.

- **Law Enforcement**
  - Strict enforcement encourages market participation.

---

## International Integration of Financial Markets

### Benefits:
- Easier access to funding globally
- More investment opportunities

### Risks:
- Financial crises can spread across countries

---

## Role of Foreign Exchange Market

international financial transactions. It plays a crucial role in enabling cross-border investments and trade.

### Functions:

# 03 — Role of Financial Institutions

## Role of Financial Institutions

Financial institutions:
- Act as intermediaries
- Reduce transaction and information costs

Financial institutions play a crucial role in resolving market imperfections and 
facilitating the flow of funds between surplus and deficit units. They help reduce 
information and transaction costs in financial markets.

### Types:
- **Depository Institutions**
  - Accept deposits and provide credit through loans and securities purchases.

- **Nondepository Institutions**
  - Generate funds from sources other than deposits but also facilitate financial intermediation.

---

## Advantages of Depository Institutions

- **Deposit Flexibility**
  - Different liquidity options. Offer accounts accommodating various liquidity needs.

- **Fund Repackaging**
  - Convert deposits into loans

- **Risk Acceptance**
  - Bear default risk

- **Credit Evaluation**
  - Assess borrower risk

- **Diversification**
  - Spread risk across many loans

---

## Commercial Banks

Commercial banks are the most dominant type  of de pository institution. They serve both surplus and deficit units through various deposit accounts and lending activities.

### Functions:
- **Deposit Accounts**
  - Offer a wide variety of deposit options for surplus units.

- **Lending**
  - Pr ovide direc t loans and purchase debt securities.

- **Risk Exposure**
  - Subject to de fault risk on loans and investments.

- **Diverse Clientele**
  - Serve households, businesses, and government agencies.

---

## Federal Funds Market

The federal funds market facilitates short-term lending between depository institutions, allowing banks with excess funds to lend to banks needing funds.

### Flow:
1. Bank with excess funds → lends
2. Market facilitates transaction
3. Bank needing funds → borrows

---

## Savings Institutions

Includes:
- Savings & Loan Associations (S&Ls)
- Savings Banks

### Characteristics:
- **Deposit Taking**
  - Accept deposits from surplus units.
  
- **Mortgage Focus**
  - Mainly residential loans

- **Ownership Structure**
  - Shareholder-owned or mutual

---

## Credit Unions

Credit unions are nonprofit depository institutions that se rve members who share  a common bond. They tend to be smaller than other depository institutions due to their membership restrictions.

### Characteristics:
- **Nonprofit**
- **Membership Restriction**
- **Smaller Size**
- **Lending Focus**
  - Mainly to members

---

## Nondepository Financial Institutions

Nondepository institutions generate funds from sources other than deposits but play a major role in financial intermediation. They include various types of institutions serving different market segments.

### Types:
- **Finance Companies**
  - Obtain funds by issuing securities and lend to individuals and small 
businesses
- **Mutual Funds**
  - Sell shares to surplus units and invest in diversified portfolios of securities.
- **Securities Firms**
  - Provide brokerage, dealer, and investment banking services. 
- **Insurance Companies**
- Provide insurance policies and invest premiums in securities.

---

## Finance Companies
Finance companies obtain funds by issuing securities and then lend those funds to 
individuals and small businesses. They focus on specific segments of the financial 
markets.

### Characteristics:
- Raise funds via securities
- Lend to individuals and small businesses
- Focus on specific market segments

---

## Mutual Funds

Mutual funds are the dominant nondepository financial institution in terms of total assets. They allow small savers to invest in diversified portfolios of securities with relatively small amounts of money.

Share Sales  
Sell shares to surplus units.

Investment Strategy  
Use funds to purchase diversified portfolios of securities.

Types  
Include stock, bond, and money market mutual funds.

Accessibility  
Allow small savers to access diversified investments.

---

## Securities Firms: Brokerage and Dealer Services

Brokerage Services  
Execute securities transactions between parties for a commission. Commission rates vary based on transaction complexity and size.

Dealer Services  
Make markets in specific securities by maintaining inventories. Income is influenced by the performance of their security portfolio.

---

## Securities Firms: Investment Banking

Securities firms often provide underwriting and advising services, commonly referred to as investment banking. These services play a crucial role in primary market activities and corporate restructuring.

Underwriting  
Place newly issued securities for corporations and government agencies.

Advisory Services  
Offer advice on mergers and corporate restructuring.

Capital Structure  
Help execute changes in a client's capital structure.

---

## Insurance Companies

Insurance companies provide policies that reduce financial burdens associated with various risks. They invest premium funds in securities, serving as important financial intermediaries.

Risk Mitigation  
Offer policies covering death, illness, and property damage.

Premium Investment  
Invest received premiums in stocks and bonds.

Financial Intermediation  
Channel funds to deficit units through investments.

Performance Link  
Overall performance tied to investment portfolio performance.

---

## Pension Funds

Pension funds manage retirement contributions from employees and employers, investing these funds in various securities. They play a significant role in financing deficit units through their investments.

Contribution Management  
Manage periodic contributions from employees and employers.

Investment Strategy  
Invest in stocks, corporate bonds, and government bonds.

Long-term Focus  
Manage funds until individuals withdraw for retirement.

Financial Intermediation  
Channel retirement savings to finance deficit units.

---

# 04 — Systemic Risk among Financial Institutions

---

## Systemic Risk in Financial Institutions

Systemic risk refers to the potential for financial problems to spread among institutions and across markets, potentially leading to a collapse of the financial system.

**Similar Investments**  
Institutions invest in similar securities.  

**Interconnected Obligations**  
Institutions rely on each other for payments.  

**Domino Effect**  
Failure of one institution can affect others.  

**System-Wide Impact**  
Can lead to widespread financial instability.  

---

## Credit Crisis of 2008–2009: Impact on Financial Institutions

The credit crisis of 2008–2009 demonstrated how systemic risk can affect various types of financial institutions through multiple channels.

**Mortgage Defaults**  
Affected institutions that purchased or invested in mortgages.  

**Mortgage-Backed Securities**  
Institutions holding these securities experienced losses.  

**Short-Term Debt Issues**  
Some institutions faced difficulties refinancing short-term debt.  

**Economic Downturn**  
Led to declines in equity values, affecting many institutions.  

---

## Regulatory Response to Financial Crises

In response to financial crises, regulators have implemented various measures to reduce systemic risk and improve the stability of financial institutions.

**Capital Requirements**  
Increased minimum capital levels for banks.  

**Stress Testing**  
Regular assessments of banks' ability to withstand economic shocks.  

**Enhanced Oversight**  
Increased supervision of systemically important institutions.  

**Resolution Planning**  
Requirements for "living wills" to manage potential failures.
